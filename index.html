About This Site

Homer's Odyssey has survived nearly three thousand years, and it keeps finding new life in modern storytelling. This site compiles and discusses the contemporary works that retell, remix, or draw inspiration from Odysseus's journey. We look at how each adaptation reinterprets the source material, what it adds to the original story, and what impact it has had on modern pop culture. Whether you're a literature enthusiast, a longtime fan of the epic, or an internet historian tracking how old stories resurface online, we hope you find something here worth exploring.

On Screen

Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey (2026)

Christopher Nolan's big-budget theatrical adaptation brings Homer's epic to IMAX screens with a starry ensemble cast. As one of the most anticipated literary adaptations in recent memory, it has reignited mainstream interest in the source text and sparked wide discussion about how a modern blockbuster handles ancient myth, monsters, and the long journey home.

O Brother, Where Art Thou? (2000)

The Coen Brothers relocate Odysseus's journey to Depression-era Mississippi, following escaped convict Ulysses Everett McGill as he makes his way home. Equal parts comedy, folk-music showcase, and loose mythological retelling, the film remains a touchstone example of how the Odyssey's bones can support a story set almost anywhere.

In Music

EPIC: The Musical

Jorge Rivera-Herrans's sung-through musical retells the entire Odyssey across a series of thematic "sagas," blending genres from rock to R&B to orchestral pop. Released largely through online platforms, it has become a genuine internet phenomenon, introducing a new generation to the epic through viral clips and fan animations.

You can read more about the project on its official website.

On the Page

Circe — Madeline Miller

Miller shifts the focus away from Odysseus entirely, centering the witch Circe and expanding her brief appearance in Homer into a full life story. The novel is often credited with helping drive the recent wave of mythology retellings from women's perspectives.

Ithaca — Claire North

North's novel stays on Ithaca itself, imagining the years Odysseus was away through the eyes of Penelope and the women around her. It reframes the "waiting" half of the Odyssey as a story of political survival in its own right.

Common Threads in These Retellings

  • Shifting narrative focus to overlooked or minor characters
  • Giving voice to female perspectives largely silent in the original text
  • Questioning the heroism of Odysseus himself
  • Using myth to comment on contemporary themes like power and survival

In Games

Hades / Hades II — Supergiant Games

While centered on the house of Hades rather than Odysseus directly, both games draw deeply from the same well of Greek myth that shaped the Odyssey, populating their underworlds with gods, monsters, and journeys of return. Their success has helped fuel renewed mainstream interest in Greek mythology across games, books, and social media alike.
